Questions, answered.

What PocketPans are, how the 4-in-1 system works, and what to know before you bake.

The basics

What is PocketPans?

PocketPans is a fillable silicone bakeware system made of pans and matching inserts. The insert molds a pocket into the batter or dough while it bakes, so you can fill cupcakes, mini pizzas and pies without coring or cutting them. There are two sets: PocketCakes for cupcakes and muffins, and PocketPies for personal pizzas, pot pies and hand pies.

What does 4-in-1 mean?

PocketPans bakeware can be used four ways: the pan on its own, the insert on its own to make mini bakes, the pan and insert together to bake a ready-made pocket, or the pan and insert together to bake two flavors in one bake, with one batter in the pan and a second in the insert's space.

Do I have to core or cut the food to fill it?

No. When you bake with the pan and insert together, the insert forms the pocket as the food bakes. After baking you lift the insert out and fill the pocket with cream, jam, sauce, cheese or any other filling.

How do I bake a filled cupcake or pie?

Fill each cavity with batter, dough or pie crust. Press the silicone insert in and snap it to the pan, with the handle staying above the surface. Bake, then lift the insert out, ideally while the food is still warm, to reveal the pocket. Fill it and serve. Each recipe on the recipes page follows the same place, insert, bake pattern.

PocketCakes and PocketPies

What comes in the PocketCakes set?

The PocketCakes set includes three silicone pans with four cavities each, which makes twelve cupcakes or muffins per batch, plus twelve matching inserts. It is the standard-size set.

What comes in the PocketPies set?

The PocketPies set includes three silicone pans with three larger cavities each, which makes nine personal bakes per batch, plus nine matching inserts. It is the personal-size set.

Should I choose PocketCakes or PocketPies?

Choose PocketCakes for standard cupcakes, muffins and mini desserts such as cheesecakes, egg bites, mousse cups and tarts. Choose PocketPies for personal deep-dish pizzas, pot pies, hand pies, quiches, calzones and stuffed bread bowls.

Can I use the pan without the insert?

Yes. The pan works on its own as a flexible silicone mold, and the inserts can also be used by themselves to make mini bakes. Using them together is what creates the fillable pocket.

Can I bake with just the inserts?

Yes. The inserts from either PocketCakes or PocketPies can be used by themselves to make mini bakes. Fill the insert with batter, set it on a baking sheet and bake it as you normally would. The pan is not needed.

Care and storage

What are PocketPans made of?

PocketPans are made of food-grade silicone that is BPA-free and non-stick.

How hot can PocketPans go in the oven?

PocketPans are heat-safe up to 450°F.

Are PocketPans dishwasher safe?

Yes. PocketPans are dishwasher safe. They are also oven safe and freezer safe.

How do I store PocketPans?

The pans nest into each other, so a full set takes up the footprint of a single pan instead of a full 12-cup pan. The inserts also stack on their own, so all 12 PocketCakes inserts or all 9 PocketPies inserts fit in the footprint of one.

Can I stack PocketPans pans?

Yes, for storage. The pans nest into one another to save space. They cannot be stacked while baking, so bake with the pans in a single layer.

Do the inserts stack too?

Yes. The inserts stack on their own, independent of the pans, so 12 PocketCakes inserts or 9 PocketPies inserts take up about the space of one.

Baking with PocketPans

What can I fill them with?

Anything that fits a pocket. Sweet fillings include cream, custard, jam, ganache, buttercream, pie filling and peanut butter and jelly. Savory fillings include chili, soup, pot pie filling, pizza sauce and cheese.

What can I bake with PocketCakes and PocketPies?

PocketCakes suit cream-filled cupcakes, jam muffins, mini coffee cakes, mini cheesecakes, egg bites, mousse cups and tarts. PocketPies suit personal deep-dish pizzas, mini chicken pot pies, chili cornbread cups, hand pies, quiches, calzones and bread bowls. See the recipes page for step-by-step ideas.

How long do I bake them?

Use your recipe's usual time and temperature as the starting point, and check that the food is done before you remove it. For pot pies, the recipes pre-bake the crust for 5 to 7 minutes, then remove the insert, fill, top and finish baking.

How do I make a personal deep-dish pizza?

Press pizza dough into the cavity about three-quarters of the way up the sidewall, then press the insert in firmly to form the pocket. Bake until the crust is set and golden, remove the insert while the crust is warm, add sauce, cheese and toppings, and return it to the oven until the cheese melts and the toppings are cooked through.
